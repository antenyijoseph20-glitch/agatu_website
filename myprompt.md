# MASTER PROJECT PROMPT
## Agatu Geographic Intelligence, Community Development, Public Safety and Investment Platform

### 1. Project Vision

We are jointly exploring and developing a long-term digital platform intended to support the geographical understanding, community development, public safety awareness, environmental knowledge, economic visibility and investment potential of Agatu Local Government Area, Benue State, Nigeria.

The project is initiated by an indigene of Agatu who believes that the area has important geographical, environmental, agricultural, natural and economic potential that deserves better documentation, responsible visibility and structured engagement with government institutions, development partners, researchers and investors.

The project must investigate this potential rather than assume that every opportunity, resource or claim is already established.

The long-term ambition is to create a credible, professionally designed and responsibly governed digital platform that can eventually be proposed for adoption, collaboration or integration by appropriate government authorities and development institutions.

Until formal authorisation is obtained, the platform must be presented as an independently developed initiative proposed for institutional collaboration, not as an official government system.

### 2. Your Role as My Mentor and Technical Adviser

Act as my:

- Senior software architect.
- GIS and geospatial systems adviser.
- Digital public infrastructure consultant.
- Security and privacy adviser.
- Research and feasibility analyst.
- Product strategist.
- Investment-information systems adviser.
- Project manager and critical mentor.

Do not merely agree with my ideas.

Challenge assumptions, identify weaknesses, explain risks, compare alternatives and recommend better approaches where necessary.

When an idea is technically possible but legally restricted, financially unrealistic, operationally difficult or dependent on government authority, explain that clearly.

Teach me progressively so that I understand the decisions behind the project rather than merely copying code.

### 3. Major Areas to Investigate

Investigate the feasibility and requirements of the following interconnected areas:

**A. Geographic and Boundary Intelligence**
- Official LGA boundaries and neighbouring local government areas.
- The ten Agatu wards.
- Communities, settlements, villages and recognised administrative structures.
- Rivers, waterways, roads, bridges and transport connections.
- Buildings, properties, markets and public infrastructure.
- GIS mapping, GPS data collection, boundary verification and geographical records.
- Comparison of OpenStreetMap, Google Maps and other suitable geospatial technologies.

Never invent boundary coordinates or present approximate locations as authoritative boundaries.

**B. Community Safety and Security Awareness**
- Community incident reporting.
- Verification and classification of reports.
- Geographical awareness of reported incidents.
- Appropriate emergency contacts and institutional referral procedures.
- Controlled access to sensitive information.
- Community alerts and public safety information.
- Coordination requirements with legally authorised security institutions.

Do not design an unauthorised surveillance, intelligence-gathering or enforcement system. Protect informants, residents and vulnerable communities. Never expose sensitive security movements, personal information or operational details on public maps.

**C. Natural Resources and Environmental Intelligence**
- Documented mineral occurrences and geological information.
- Agricultural resources and land suitability.
- Water resources, rivers and wetlands.
- Forests, vegetation and environmental assets.
- Environmental risks, erosion, flooding and other hazards.
- Opportunities for responsible resource development.

Distinguish between confirmed deposits, geological indications, historical reports, unverified community knowledge and speculative claims. Investigate ownership, licensing, environmental protection and regulatory requirements.

**D. Investment and Economic Development**
- Agriculture and agro-processing.
- Markets, trade and commercial activity.
- Transport and logistics.
- Tourism and cultural heritage.
- Infrastructure development.
- Renewable energy and other feasible economic opportunities.
- Business registration and approved business directories.
- Investor enquiries and institutional engagement.

Investigate what information investors actually require, what can be independently verified and how the platform could present credible opportunities without making investment guarantees.

**E. Government and Development Intelligence**
- Public infrastructure directories.
- Education and healthcare facilities.
- Community needs and development gaps.
- Project documentation and progress reporting.
- Publicly available government information.
- Development partner and NGO engagement.
- Evidence-based reporting and data visualisation.

**F. Community Participation**
- Resident and business submissions.
- Community verification and correction procedures.
- Participation by traditional institutions and relevant community representatives.
- Authorised government officer workflows.
- Feedback and transparent record management.
- Accessibility for residents with limited internet connectivity.

### 4. Feasibility and Barrier Assessment

Before recommending implementation, investigate:

1. Legal authority and government jurisdiction.
2. Land rights, boundary disputes and property-registration restrictions.
3. Data protection, privacy and cybersecurity obligations.
4. Security risks and possible misuse of geographical information.
5. Availability and reliability of official data.
6. GIS and GPS accuracy requirements.
7. Internet connectivity and mobile-device limitations.
8. Technology and hosting costs.
9. Mapping-provider licensing and usage costs.
10. Maintenance and long-term operational responsibilities.
11. Funding and sustainability.
12. Community trust, participation and consent.
13. Government adoption and institutional collaboration.
14. Environmental and social safeguards.
15. Skills and professional expertise required.

For each major feature, classify it as:

- Feasible with current resources.
- Feasible with additional skills or funding.
- Dependent on external data or partnerships.
- Dependent on government or regulatory authorisation.
- High-risk or unsuitable without further safeguards.
- Not currently recommended.

Explain the reason for each classification.

### 5. Research and Evidence Standards

Use reliable, relevant and preferably primary sources.

Prioritise:
- Nigerian federal and state government institutions.
- Official geographical and administrative records.
- Relevant regulatory agencies.
- Recognised geological and environmental sources.
- Peer-reviewed research.
- Reputable development organisations.
- Verified community information with transparent provenance.

For important claims, record:
- What is being claimed.
- The source.
- The date of the information.
- Its geographical coverage.
- Verification status.
- Limitations and uncertainty.

Never manufacture statistics, mineral deposits, investment opportunities, boundary data or government endorsements.

Clearly separate verified facts, reasonable estimates, hypotheses and proposals.

### 6. Architecture and Technology Principles

Preserve and assess the existing Agatu LGA Flask website before proposing major changes.

Evaluate suitable technologies, including:
- Python and Flask.
- PostgreSQL and PostGIS.
- Leaflet and OpenStreetMap.
- Google Maps services where justified.
- GPS-enabled mobile data collection.
- Secure administrative dashboards.
- Role-based access control.
- Auditing and record verification.
- Appropriate APIs and data exchange formats.

Avoid unnecessary complexity, expensive dependencies and premature microservices.

Recommend an architecture that can begin modestly and grow responsibly.

### 7. Governance, Privacy and Security

Design the platform around:
- Clear ownership and accountability.
- Least-privilege access.
- Separate public and restricted datasets.
- Human verification and approval.
- Secure authentication.
- Audit trails.
- Data retention and correction procedures.
- Responsible disclosure of geographical information.
- Community protection and appropriate institutional oversight.

Never imply that the platform itself possesses government enforcement powers.

### 8. Development Methodology

Do not rush into coding.

Work through these stages:

**Stage 1 — Vision and Discovery:** Clarify the problem, beneficiaries, objectives and boundaries of the project.

**Stage 2 — Research and Feasibility:** Investigate Agatu's geography, development needs, available resources, opportunities, risks and institutional environment.

**Stage 3 — Stakeholder and Governance Analysis:** Identify relevant government agencies, traditional institutions, communities, technical partners and potential investors.

**Stage 4 — Requirements Engineering:** Develop detailed functional and non-functional requirements.

**Stage 5 — System Architecture:** Design the database, application modules, mapping architecture, security controls and integrations.

**Stage 6 — Project Prioritisation:** Separate the minimum viable product from later expansion phases.

**Stage 7 — Implementation Planning:** Estimate costs, resources, skills, dependencies, risks and milestones.

**Stage 8 — Approval and Development:** Implement only after the relevant documents and decisions have been reviewed and approved.

**Stage 9 — Testing and Validation:** Test functionality, geographical accuracy, privacy, security, accessibility and reliability.

**Stage 10 — Pilot and Institutional Engagement:** Develop a controlled pilot, gather community feedback and pursue appropriate institutional collaboration.

### 10. The First Assignment

Begin by conducting a serious project discovery and feasibility investigation.

Do not start coding.

First establish:
1. What Agatu needs most.
2. What reliable information already exists.
3. What geographical, environmental and economic opportunities can be substantiated.
4. What threats and development barriers need attention.
5. What the platform can realistically achieve.
6. What requires external authorisation or collaboration.
7. What should be built first.
8. What the project may cost and how it could be sustained.

Finish the first investigation with a critical assessment, a prioritised opportunity list, a risk register, unresolved research questions and a recommended sequence of work.

The ultimate goal is not simply to create a website. It is to develop a credible, useful, secure and sustainable digital resource that can help Agatu become better documented, safer, more visible and better positioned for responsible development.

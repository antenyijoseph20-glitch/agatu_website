## 3. Requirements

Road register: corridor name and variants, start/end coordinates, chainage/length, ward/community, road class/surface, bridges/culverts, project history, funder, implementing agency, contractor, financial stages, planned/actual dates, current condition, seasonal accessibility, defects, inspection evidence and maintenance responsibility.

Transport register: bus terminals, public transport operators, routes, river crossings, jetties, landing sites, boat services and accessibility.

## 4. Key risk: corridor overlap

Do not double-count contracts or treat different road names as separate physical projects without mapping route, chainage, scope and funding package. Preserve separate project records until evidence establishes parent/child or overlap relationships.

## 5. Research gaps

Obtain engineering drawings, bills of quantities, contracts, variation orders, chainages, maps, payment certificates, completion certificates, current road-condition surveys, bridge inspections, NIWA records and transport operator/community interviews.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

A road's length, route, contractor, cost and status must be source-backed. Historical progress must be date-labelled. Current condition requires field inspection. Tender/flag-off/award/payment/completion remain distinct.

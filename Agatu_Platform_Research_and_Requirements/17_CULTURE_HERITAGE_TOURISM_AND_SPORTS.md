# Sector 17 — Culture, Heritage, Tourism, Arts and Sports

**Status:** Working research baseline; community consultation and permissions required.

## 1. Oral literature and music

ABU doctoral thesis *A Study of the Form, Content and Performance of Agatu Songs* by Jerry Idu Samuel, March 2015, based on recordings/interviews/fieldwork in 2010, documents:
- Akpanchala: praise, dirges, history, moral instruction and social commentary.
- Okpeke: praise, satire, social correction and community values.
- Ayon: social criticism, moral instruction, historical memory and encouragement.

The thesis identifies fieldwork locations including Ogbaulu, Enungba and Aila and notes decline/death of some performers/instrumentalists. Fresh fieldwork is required to establish present practice.

## 2. Fishing festival and Ochulo pond

Reports describe a joint Egba–Ologba fishing festival around Ochulo fishpond in March 2022 following a peace process over pond ownership. Communities reportedly agreed to share the pond and hold an annual joint festival. A 2022 Agatu Fishing Festival announcement invited traditional/community and government stakeholders.

This is a cultural and peacebuilding lead, not proof that annual editions continued. The pond has conflict history; do not publish ownership claims or promote as a tourist destination without community consent, safety assessment and legal clarification.

## 3. Traditional institutions and practices

Benue heritage portal describes Eje' Alekwu as an important Idoma traditional festival. Its general descriptions must not be copied as proof of every Agatu community's specific customs.

Research Agatu's own traditional institutions, local practices, languages/dialects, festivals, marriage/naming/burial customs, conflict-resolution traditions, cultural associations and diaspora groups. Respect custodianship and restricted knowledge.

## 4. Tourism and natural attractions

Potential assets include River Benue/waterways, fishing traditions, agricultural landscapes, local food culture, festivals, music and crafts. A 2019 baseline describes riverine/fadama, highland and western forest environments. No authoritative comprehensive Agatu tourist-site register was found.

Before promotion verify custodianship/ownership, access, safety, environmental condition, community consent, facilities, conservation and legal status.

## 5. Arts, crafts and creative industries

Investigate clothing, weaving, adornment, pottery, carving, basketry, instruments, food preservation, fishing equipment, performers, artisans and creative businesses. Record only practices confirmed in Agatu and whether practitioners consent to promotion.

## 6. Sports

- Community-linked website reports Enbago Football Tournament has operated for 16 years and describes a 2024 edition; verify organisers, dates and history.
- Same source reports support for Samson Football Tournament in Ogwule Ogbaulu; verify.
- AIT reported Agatu reached Benue State Governor's Cup final in Jan 2025 and lost to Vandeikya on penalties after 0–0.
- 2020 interview discussed ambition for an Agatu football hub/academy; aspiration only, not proof of completed academy or stadium.

Research football, volleyball, athletics, wrestling, traditional games, facilities, clubs, competitions, athletes, coaching, scholarships and youth programmes.

## 7. Requirements

Separate registers:
1. Cultural Heritage.
2. Oral History and Language Archive.
3. Traditional Institutions.
4. Tourism and Natural Attractions.
5. Arts, Crafts and Creative Businesses.
6. Sports and Recreation.

Each record: source, date, community/ward, custodian, consent/permission, verification status, language, publication restrictions, recording provenance and correction history.

## 8. Cultural safety

Do not publish restricted songs, rituals, sacred sites, family knowledge or identifiable interviews without appropriate permission. Preserve differing oral accounts rather than declaring one version true without evidence.

## 9. Research gaps

Conduct interviews with cultural custodians, traditional rulers, performers, sports organisers, artists and relevant agencies; verify site inventory and whether annual festivals continued.

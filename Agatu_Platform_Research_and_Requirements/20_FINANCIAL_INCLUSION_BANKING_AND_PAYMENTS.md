# Sector 20 — Financial Inclusion, Banking, Payments and Local Economic Infrastructure

**Status:** Working research baseline; current Agatu access-point inventory incomplete.

## 1. Historical agent-banking access

A 2022 peer-reviewed study, *An Empirical Analysis of the Impact of Agency Banking on Financial Inclusion in Benue State, Nigeria*, reproduces First Bank Financial Inclusion Team records showing 15 First Bank agents in Agatu as at June 2020. The study marks Agatu as having no conventional bank branch in its classification at that time.

This is historical evidence, not a current agent count. It does not show ward distribution or continuing operation. Study identifies statewide challenges including cash shortages, insecurity, network failures and limited financial literacy; these are not Agatu-specific measurements.

## 2. Novus MFB state agent programme

Benue State announced on 30 Sep 2024 that Novus MFB was beginning induction training for banking agents under a 3,000-agent initiative across 276 council wards, aiming for services within a five-kilometre radius. Target groups included MSMEs, market women, traders, vulnerable groups and displaced people; announcement also mentioned loans, grants and insurance for smallholder farmers.

This is a programme announcement, not proof of deployment in Agatu. Verify recruitment, training, agent placement, licensing, activation, cash/e-float, service quality and beneficiary outcomes.

Published contact lead: 07000066887632; Agent.banking@novusmfb.com. Verify current details before use.

## 3. Financial inclusion evidence

EFInA Access to Financial Services in Nigeria 2023:
- National inclusion increased from adjusted 68% in 2020 to 74% in 2023.
- Formal inclusion rose from 57% to 64%.
- Rural formal inclusion 51%, urban 75%.
- Benue among states with high reliance on informal channels and among seven states with largest numbers of financially excluded adults.

These are national/state-level results, not Agatu-specific rates. National mobile-money users reported network reliability, failed transactions, trust and knowledge barriers.

Assess access, usage, quality and impact separately.

## 4. Cooperatives and savings groups

Benue Ministry of Cooperatives and Rural Development has responsibilities for cooperative policy, registration/supervision, community development and rural empowerment.

Published guidelines refer to Benue State Co-operative Society Law 1976 and require at least ten qualified individuals for a primary cooperative application. Verify current law and administrative procedure.

2025 state reform plan proposes validation/profiling of 1,000 cooperatives. No Agatu allocation or beneficiary list established.

Investigate farmer, fisher, livestock, women/youth, trader, artisan and savings cooperatives. Distinguish registered cooperative from informal savings group.

## 5. Agricultural finance

- CBN Agricultural Credit Guarantee Scheme Fund (ACGSF), operating since 1978, encourages agricultural lending and guarantees up to 75% of eligible net amount in default subject to rules. Farmers apply through participating banks; Fund is not a direct lender or grant.
- NIRSAL facilitates agricultural finance/risk-sharing; not a bank directly lending to farmers.
- Bank of Industry offers business finance programmes including MSME and women-led enterprise support.
- CBN MSME interventions include historical/existing programmes; current application status and eligibility must be checked.

Research Agatu beneficiaries, loans approved/disbursed, lender, sector, repayment outcomes and privacy-safe aggregated results.

## 6. Benue microcredit and financial literacy proposal

Benue 2025 Business-Enabling Reform Action Plan includes proposed ₦3bn microcredit and financial-literacy programme, with Novus MFB and other partners; targets 5,000 SMEs reached with microcredit and 10,000 entrepreneurs trained by 2026.

Targets/estimated cost are not proof of release, lending or Agatu allocation. Obtain approved guidelines, releases, participating lenders, criteria, beneficiary distribution, amounts disbursed, repayment/default aggregates and training outcomes.

## 7. Business registration and formalisation

Benue reform plan proposes Digital Business Registration and Licensing System, with target operationalisation Q3 2025 and 50% increase in registered businesses by 2026. Current operational status and Agatu usage not verified.

Distinguish:
- CAC federal business/company registration.
- State/local licensing, permits and tax obligations.
- Voluntary platform business listing.

A platform listing does not imply CAC registration, licence, tax compliance or financial vetting.

## 8. Digital payments and consumer protection

Investigate POS/agent banking, mobile money/payment service banks, bank transfers, USSD, cash/agent liquidity, transaction charges, failed/reversed transactions, fraud, accessibility and complaints.

Never collect PINs, BVNs, passwords, full card details or OTPs. If platform payments are later approved, use a regulated provider; do not store payment credentials.

## 9. Requirements

Registers:
1. Financial access points.
2. Cooperatives and savings groups.
3. Agricultural finance schemes.
4. MSME finance and support.
5. Digital payments/consumer protection.
6. Financial literacy/inclusion.

Access-point fields: provider, regulatory category, service type, ward/settlement, location accuracy, operating status and last verification, hours, services, public contact, source. Do not publish sensitive cash balances or security arrangements.

## 10. Research gaps

Ward-by-ward current bank/agent/POS/mobile-money survey; current conventional branch status; Novus agent deployment list; cooperative register; agricultural loan beneficiaries; microcredit implementation; CAC/business registration data; financial literacy and consumer complaint evidence.

## 11. Acceptance criteria

Historical counts are date-labelled. Programme targets are not described as achievements. Agatu-specific rates are not inferred from state statistics. Sensitive financial and personal data remains private.

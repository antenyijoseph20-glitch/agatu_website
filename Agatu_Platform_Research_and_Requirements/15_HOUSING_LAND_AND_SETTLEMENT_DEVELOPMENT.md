# Sector 15 — Housing, Land Administration and Settlement Development

**Status:** Working research baseline; legal and cadastral information restricted by default.

## 1. Institutions and BENGIS

BENGIS describes itself as a government-owned land/geospatial service agency. Its published scope includes surveying, mapping, town planning, land administration, solid minerals, valuation, sites/services and development control. Its stated digital scope includes GNSS, drone/LiDAR surveys, cadastral/topographic maps, geodatabases, digitising historical land records, C-of-O processing and revenue management. These are published institutional descriptions, not independent proof that every online function is currently operational.

BENGIS FAQ says services include land registration, cadastral mapping, title verification, GIS mapping and property valuation; it also indicates that online applications, GIS access and payments are still being digitalised. Contact lead: bengis@benuestate.gov.ng; +234 708 064 0436; BENGIS Service Centre, Ministry of Lands, Makurdi. Verify before relying on details.

Benue Ministry of Lands and Survey describes land policy, acquisition, planning, survey, allocation, title registration, boundary delimitation, state maps, compensation and LGA liaison.

Town Planning functions include general/detailed planning, settlement layouts/upgrading, land acquisition, LUAC, planning reports, C-of-O approvals, traffic/land-use planning, urban renewal, development control, settlement monitoring and conservation.

Survey Department functions include plot surveys/beaconing, layouts, disputed plot surveys, government engineering surveys, control pillars, interstate boundaries, inter-LGA/district boundaries and survey coordinate storage.

## 2. Legal framework

Land Use Act 1978 places land in each state in the Governor's trust, with urban/non-urban administration and customary/statutory rights governed by the Act. Do not simplify community/customary land as simply “government-owned.” Legal title, customary occupation, survey plan, allocation, land use and physical occupation are distinct.

The platform must not issue titles, adjudicate ownership, settle land disputes or independently certify ownership. Any authorised integration with BENGIS or land authorities requires formal permission and clear legal boundaries.

## 3. Benue–Nasarawa boundary

NBC announced a planned interstate demarcation using historical descriptions from 1916, 1919 and 1924, with an intended 2022 start. A 2024 report said work paused in April 2022 due to security, some pillars had been emplaced and some reportedly destroyed. No completed Agatu-relevant final boundary record was established.

Do not confuse Cameroon–Nigeria boundary material with Benue–Nasarawa. Obtain NBC-specific maps, field records, community sensitisation and final approvals.

## 5. Requirements

Separate:
A. Settlement/geographic system.
B. Community location/property information system (not title registry).
C. Official land administration/cadastral/title system under government authority.

Settlement record: name variants, ward/LGA, type, coordinates/accuracy, extent, population source/year, roads, facilities, historical changes, source and verification.

Land-related public information must be restricted where it includes private parcels, title documents, cadastral records, disputes or personal data. Public users cannot edit authoritative boundaries.
